# Keyword and Content Workflow

## 1. Goal

Choose queries that attract the right audience and support real business outcomes. Do not optimize for raw traffic alone.

## 2. Discovery vs mutation

Discovery may inspect:
- repository/site content;
- Search Console;
- analytics;
- SERPs;
- keyword tools;
- competitors;
- internal links;
- existing rankings;
- sitemap and routes.

Keyword-targeted mutation includes:
- title;
- H1/headings;
- body copy;
- FAQ copy;
- anchors;
- slug;
- content-driven meta description;
- new keyword-targeted pages/articles.

Safe technical corrections can proceed independently under authorized scope. Keyword-targeted content changes require an approved page decision.

## 3. Evidence order

Prefer:
1. first-party Search Console query/page/country/device data;
2. analytics/conversion/CRM evidence;
3. Google Keyword Planner or equivalent first-party demand data;
4. Google Trends for relative trend/seasonality only;
5. live SERP inspection;
6. reputable third-party keyword tools;
7. site/business relevance.

Never fabricate missing metrics.

## 5. Research sequence

1. understand offer, audience, geography, conversions, language;
2. inventory current pages;
3. extract seed topics from products/features/problems/jobs-to-be-done;
4. collect first-party query performance;
5. expand queries from tools, SERPs, related searches, questions, local modifiers;
6. inspect live SERPs for shortlisted queries;
7. identify real organic competitors;
8. run competitor content/keyword gaps;
9. classify intent and rewarded page type;
10. cluster same-intent variants;
11. detect cannibalization;
12. map one primary cluster to one preferred destination;
13. prioritize by business fit, evidence-backed demand, feasibility, trend, current authority, and conversion potential.

## 6. Intent classes

Use at least:
- informational;
- commercial investigation;
- transactional;
- navigational/brand;
- local;
- mixed.

A page should normally have one dominant intent with supporting sub-intents. Do not stuff unrelated queries into one page.

## 7. SERP evidence

Before a high-impact target decision, inspect the current target-market SERP when possible.

Record:
- dominant page types;
- mixed intent;
- result features;
- freshness;
- local/shopping/video/image behavior;
- recurring subtopics;
- real ranking competitors.

A competitor ranking for a keyword is evidence to investigate, not proof the site should target it.

## 8. Cannibalization

Flag when multiple URLs compete for substantially the same intent and neither has a deliberate distinct role.

Do not “fix cannibalization” by merging pages automatically.

Propose:
- differentiate intent;
- consolidate;
- canonicalize;
- redirect;
- internal-link preference;
- noindex only when justified.

URL consolidation is approval-required.

## 9. Decision packet

For each page show:
- URL/planned URL;
- page type;
- current role;
- primary keyword/topic;
- secondary cluster;
- intent;
- evidence;
- business fit;
- SERP-fit;
- cannibalization risk;
- recommended action;
- content gaps;
- approval: `approve | revise | skip`.

For site-wide work, batch all pages in one table.

## 10. New content

Every proposed page/article must have independent value.

Require:
- unique reader problem;
- clear intent;
- primary cluster;
- overlap check;
- information gain;
- outline;
- source/fact requirements;
- internal links;
- CTA;
- update/freshness risk.

Do not create pages solely because a keyword exists.

## 11. Programmatic SEO

Allowed only when each page provides useful, materially distinct value.

Refuse or flag:
- location pages with only city-name substitution;
- mass “X alternatives” pages with no original analysis;
- templated integration pages without real integration details;
- AI-generated thin glossaries;
- scraped or synonymized pages;
- doorway pages.

Scaled content abuse is method-neutral: human or AI generation does not make thin pages acceptable.

## 12. Content quality

Look for:
- first-hand evidence;
- specific examples;
- original data;
- real screenshots/demos;
- explicit limitations;
- accurate citations;
- current facts;
- expert review where needed;
- useful comparison criteria.

Flag commodity copy that could be swapped onto a competitor site with little change.

## 13. Opportunity scores

Any custom score is an internal prioritization model, not a Google metric.

If inputs are incomplete:
- disclose completeness;
- do not pretend precision;
- avoid ranking “best” based on invented values.
